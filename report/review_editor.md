**1. Replace the abstract (line 16).** D59 requires the SIDED sentence and one N2 sentence. Proposed text, 244 words by `wc -w`:

> AI mediators turn many people's opinions into one group statement, and participants' rankings of candidates are a natural training signal for them. If rankings favour statements that hide the group's real disagreement, a mediator optimised for approval would learn to hide it; we call this crux erasure. We present, to our knowledge, the first pre-registered test of whether participants' rankings favour it, on 721 held-out launches of the public Habermas Machine release. An open-weight LLM scorer takes the points of disagreement (cruxes) from the opinions alone and scores the share each candidate hides. Its detection of disagreement was validated against participants' pre-deliberation position ratings (balanced accuracy 0.69, 1,622 rounds); its hide/show judgement was not, because the planned author check was not done in time. By the scorer's reading, 96% of candidates hid every crux, mostly by taking a side: 82.8% of crux labels are SIDED (one side presented as the group's view). The within-round rank test gave an association that is small and sensitive to how statement length is controlled, and that cannot be separated from raters preferring statements that state their own side (pre-registered test; the predictor's hide/show contrast is not human-validated); we draw no directional conclusion about rankings. In an exploratory grid, a "keep everyone satisfied" system prompt raised the scored share of hidden cruxes in all five open-weight models, relative to "be accurate about differences" (by 0.28 to 0.37); nobody ranked these statements. The data are observational, from one study's mediator.
