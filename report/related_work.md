## 2. Related Work

**The original study found the opposite of erasure, on a different measure.** Tessler et al. [CITE:tessler2024habermas] report that, although support for the majority position rose after deliberation, revised statements overweighted the minority on an embedding stance axis (0.36 against an empirical share of 0.29). That measure is a position on an agree-disagree axis and cannot tell a statement that names a disagreement from a vague mid-point one. They also report that rankings were affected by statement length, which is why we control for it. They did not test whether rankings favour statements that hide disagreement.

**Content of AI consensus statements.** Mahajan et al. [CITE:mahajan2026commitments] find that mediator prompts seeking agreement cover minority positions much less often than prompts asking for differences (judged by LLMs only). Wan et al. [CITE:wan2026deliberative] show that LLM agents "can agree more while knowing less", and Zhu et al. [CITE:zhu2025voice] benchmark 18 LLMs and report minority under-representation; they also find that off-the-shelf LLM judges' correlations with human ratings "never exceed 0.4", which is one reason we check our scorer against a participant signal. Zaghouani [CITE:zaghouani2026accountable] proposes an unvalidated embedding formula also called "Erasure", hence our name "crux erasure score"; coverage and fairness metrics for summaries [CITE:zhang2024fairsumm; CITE:khosravani2024argument] are the closest measurement precedents. None of these tests human rankings of competing statements.

**Bridging and sycophancy.** Bridging-based ranking [CITE:wojcik2022birdwatch; CITE:ovadya2023bridging], quality-based comment ranking [CITE:revel2025representative] and consensus-seeking opinion selection [CITE:hafid2026opinion] can reduce the visibility of contested or minority views, but they rank comments or opinions, not candidate group statements. Unlike individual sycophancy [CITE:sharma2024sycophancy], no single rater is flattered in the group form; each is partly pleased by a statement that does not name the clash. Our search was keyword-based, and for several cited works we read only abstracts or partial text.
